    Caenorhabditis elegans · N2 hermaphrodite

    A whole nervous system in your pocket.

    This worm is a real wiring diagram brought to life. Every neuron and synapse comes from the electron-microscope reconstruction of the C. elegans nervous system: 300 neurons joined by thousands of synapses and gap junctions.

    What you can do

    • Plate. Stroke a worm with an eyelash, which is how biologists test touch. The nose and front make it back up; the tail makes it speed forward. Drop bacteria and watch it find them by smell.
    • Brain. Watch every neuron light up as the worm thinks. Tap one to read about it, stimulate it, or cut it out.
    • Experiments. Repeat classic laser-ablation experiments: remove the command neurons and the worm can no longer back up.
    • Relay. Race a signal across the connectome, or find the shortest route between any two cells.

    How the model works

    Each neuron is a leaky unit whose activity follows its inputs. Chemical synapses are weighted by how many contacts the micrographs show; gap junctions pull partners toward the same voltage. GABA neurons inhibit. The wiring doesn't record whether a glutamate synapse excites or inhibits, so a few pathways follow the classic circuit papers. Those rules are listed in src/brain.js.

    The forward and reverse command cells decide the direction. Head motor neurons steer. The body is a kinematic chain that follows its own head, as a worm does on agar. This is a toy model: it gets the famous behaviours right, but it is not a prediction of what a real worm would do.
